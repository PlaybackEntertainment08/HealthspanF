The inaugural honours

The Longevity 40

Forty clinicians, scientists, innovators, authors and organisations recognised for advancing evidence-based regenerative and longevity medicine.

Nominate a colleagueThe eleven categories

Nominations open 1 Oct 2026 · close 31 Jan 2027 · ceremony Sat 10 April 2027, 18:15

What it is

A list that cannot be bought

Most awards in this field are sold. An organisation invites a nomination, charges for the entry, charges again for the table at the dinner, and prints the trophy. Everyone involved knows what it is worth.

The Longevity 40 is run the other way round. There is no entry fee at any stage. Honourees attend the summit free and are not charged for the ceremony, the dinner or the citation. No sponsor may buy an honour, place a candidate, or see the shortlist before it is announced.

The panel is appointed by IASRM and then works without reference to it. No member of the organising committee sits on it. No member of the scientific committee sits on it. The panel’s decision is final and is not disclosed to the organisers until the week of the summit.

Eleven categories

What is recognised

Forty honourees are distributed across eleven categories. The panel is not obliged to fill every category, and may leave one empty rather than lower the bar.

01

Clinical Excellence in Regenerative Medicine

Sustained clinical practice with documented outcomes, honest consent and published complication rates.

02

Patient Outcomes & Safety

Work that measurably reduced harm — a registry, a protocol, a safety audit that changed practice.

03

Education & Mentorship

Teaching that produced practitioners who grade their claims. Named by those they trained.

04

Access & Community Practice

Bringing evidence-based longevity care to patients who could not otherwise reach it.

05

Emerging Practitioner of the Year

Within ten years of first registration, already doing careful work.

06

Author of the Year

A book, paper or body of writing that improved how this field explains itself.

07

Scientist of the Year

Laboratory or translational work of consequence, published and reproducible.

08

Researcher of the Year

Clinical research — a trial run properly, including one that reported a negative result.

09

Innovator of the Year

A device, assay, protocol or service that works and whose claims match its data.

10

Company of the Year

An organisation whose marketing does not outrun its evidence.

11

Manufacturing & Product Excellence

Quality systems, traceability and release testing taken seriously.

How the panel decides

The process, in full

  1. Nomination. Anyone may nominate. Self-nomination is permitted but is marked as such and weighted accordingly. Nominations are free and unlimited in number.
  2. Eligibility screen. The secretariat checks that the nominee exists, works in the field, and is not subject to a current disciplinary finding. The secretariat does not score.
  3. Referees. Each nomination names two referees who are contacted independently. A nomination with no reachable referee does not proceed.
  4. Scoring. Each panel member scores independently against the published criteria before any discussion takes place. Scores are pooled, outliers discussed, then re-scored.
  5. Recusal. A panel member with any relationship to a nominee — institutional, commercial, familial or supervisory — recuses from that category entirely.
  6. Decision. Final, unappealable, and not disclosed to the organising committee or to any sponsor until the week of the summit.

What the panel looks for

  • Evidence that matches the claim
  • Work that others can verify
  • Complications reported, not hidden
  • Patients served, not just billed
  • Students who outgrew the teacher
  • A reputation held among peers rather than in advertising
