The format

Six minutes is not a short lecture

It is a different form. Six minutes forces a speaker to choose one claim, show the data behind it, and state its tier. There is no room for a review of the field, and that is the point.

A timer runs on the lectern and on the hall screen. At six minutes the microphone is cut — courteously, and without exception, including for the organisers. The nine minutes that follow belong to the floor, and the chair takes questions from the back of the hall first.

Slides are submitted by 25 March 2027 and are checked for one thing only: that every empirical claim carries a tier.

Each round, in minutes

0 – 36
Six speakers, six minutes each, back to back, no introductions between them.
36 – 45
All six return to the stage. Nine minutes of questions, put to the panel rather than to one speaker.

Thirty talks across the five rounds. Speakers are selected from submitted abstracts by the scientific committee; see abstracts & submissions.

The five rounds

What each round covers

Round I · 09:05 – 09:50

Measuring Ageing in the Real Clinic

Biological-age reports and what to tell the patient who brings one in · ApoB over LDL-C · HbA1c and hs-CRP as the two cheapest useful numbers · VO₂max estimation without a metabolic cart · grip strength in a busy OPD · the one-page longevity work-up.

Round II · 09:50 – 10:35

The Indian Plate

Vegetarian protein sufficiency and leucine thresholds · rice, roti and glycaemic load · religious fasting as an intermittent-fasting model · ghee, seed oils and the evidence actually available · spices beyond the mechanism slide · the thali, reconstructed.

Round III · 10:50 – 11:35

Regenerative Practice: Cases, Complications & Consent

A PRP case that failed, presented by the clinician who did it · BMAC in primary ovarian insufficiency · the regulatory position on exosomes · adverse events and how they are reported · stem-cell tourism and the patient who returns with a complication.

Round IV · 11:35 – 12:20

Traditions Under the Evidence Lens

Yoga trials and their control arms · pranayama and heart-rate variability · rasayana preparations and what has been measured · meditation and cognitive outcomes · dinacharya as circadian hygiene · loneliness, purpose and mortality.

Round V · 14:45 – 15:30

Women’s Healthspan

Ovarian ageing and the AMH conversation · perimenopause in general practice · bone health after 40 · the pelvic floor, unembarrassed · PCOS across the lifespan · the thin endometrium and regenerative approaches to it.

30

talks of six minutes across five rounds. Speakers announced January 2027.

Put yourself forward

For speakers

What we ask of you

Choose the single thing you want the room to remember. Six minutes will hold one claim with its evidence, or three claims with none of it.

Lower-right corner, in the template supplied. Where a slide mixes tiers, the lowest applicable tier governs. The house rule in full.

Equity, advisory roles, speaking fees, and ownership of any clinic delivering what you are about to discuss. It takes ten seconds and it buys you the room’s trust for the remaining five minutes and fifty.

Round III exists for it. A complication presented honestly by the clinician who caused it teaches more than a series of successes, and the committee weights such submissions accordingly.

Abstracts close 31 January 2027. Decisions by 20 February. Final slides by 25 March. Speakers must be registered delegates by 28 February to keep their slot.
