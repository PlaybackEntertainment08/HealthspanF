Part I · The Preamble

WE, THE RESEARCHERS, SCIENTISTS, INNOVATORS, INDUSTRIALISTS AND PRACTITIONERS OF LONGEVITY & REGENERATIVE MEDICINE,

having solemnly resolved to constitute our discipline into an EVIDENCE-LED, ETHICAL, EQUITABLE and HUMANE science, and to secure to every patient JUSTICE of honest counsel, every claim graded by its evidence; LIBERTY of informed consent where the data is thin; EQUALITY of access beyond the reach of wealth alone; and FRATERNITY, assuring the dignity of the patient and the healthspan of the nation;

in our Summit at New Delhi this eleventh day of April, 2027, do

HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CHARTER.

Article I

The House Rule

Every claim made from this stage carries its evidence tier on the slide. Without exception, and including the organisers.

The tier is printed in the lower-right corner of every slide that makes an empirical claim, in the delegate app beside every abstract, and on the card at every Experience Lounge station. Chairs are briefed to ask for it when it is missing.

Evidence before enthusiasm.

Hear a claim presented above its grade? Say so from the floor. That is not an interruption of the programme. It is the programme.

The four tiers

Tier I
Human randomised trial. Randomised controlled evidence in humans, or a meta-analysis of such trials, reporting the outcome being claimed.
Tier II
Human observational. Cohort, case-control, registry or large case-series data in humans. Association, not causation.
Tier III
Animal. In-vivo animal evidence, including the long-lived model organisms. Not yet shown in people.
Tier IV
Mechanism alone. In-vitro, pathway or theoretical reasoning. A hypothesis worth testing, and nothing more.

Where a claim mixes tiers, the lowest applicable tier governs the slide.

Part II · The Draft Statements

Twelve statements, to be argued

These are drafts circulated to registered delegates in February 2027. Each is read aloud, argued, amended if an amendment carries, and voted on in Panel II on Sunday.

No claim of clinical benefit should be communicated to a patient, a colleague or the public without an accompanying statement of the strength of evidence supporting it. Where that evidence is mechanistic or animal-derived only, it should be described as such in plain language.

Consent for an intervention supported by Tier III or Tier IV evidence should record, in writing and in the patient’s language, that human outcome data is absent, what is known of the risks, what the realistic alternatives are, and what the patient will pay.

Advertising of regenerative or longevity interventions to the public should not use before-and-after imagery, testimonials or survival language for any claim graded below Tier II, and should name the practitioner responsible for the clinical content.

A biological-age figure should never be reported to a patient without its measurement error, the cohort it was trained on, and a statement of whether changing it has been shown to change any clinical outcome.

Practitioners offering cell, exosome or orthobiologic therapies should report serious adverse events to a national registry within thirty days, and should maintain a complication log open to audit by the referring physician.

Peptides, exosome preparations and cell products marketed for indications without Tier I or Tier II support should not be dispensed from the same consultation in which they are first recommended, and should not be sold at a margin by the prescribing clinician.

Whole-body imaging and multi-cancer early-detection panels should be offered only with a documented plan for incidental findings, and with a quantified statement of the false-positive rate in a population of the patient’s risk.

Protocols, reference ranges and risk calculators developed in Western cohorts should be stated as such when applied to Indian patients, and adapted where Indian data exists. Where it does not exist, generating it is a professional obligation of this community.

Any intervention this community recommends as standard of care should have a stated low-cost pathway, or be described honestly as a discretionary intervention available to those who can pay.

Traditional Indian practices should be held to the same evidence grading as any other intervention — neither exempted from it out of deference, nor dismissed without examining the trials that exist.

Speakers, authors and panel members should declare financial interests on the opening slide and in the published abstract, including equity, advisory roles, speaking fees and ownership of the clinic delivering the intervention discussed.

Continuous biometric data collected in a longevity practice is medical data. It should be stored under the same protections as a clinical record, should not be sold, and the patient should be able to export and delete it.

These are draftsRegistered delegates receive the circulated text in February 2027 and may submit amendments in writing until 31 March. Amendments may also be moved from the floor during Panel II.

Part III · Procedure

Read. Argued. Amended. Signed.

  1. The consensus statements are read from the floor, one by one, by a delegate drawn at random rather than by a chair.
  2. They are argued: evidence, consent, advertising, regulation, and what a clinician owes a patient when the data is thin. Three minutes for, three against, then the floor.
  3. Delegates vote on each by show of card. A simple majority carries an amendment; two-thirds carries a statement into the Charter.
  4. Signed on Sunday afternoon by those who choose to be bound by them. Signing is voluntary and is recorded by name and institution.

After Delhi

The adopted Charter is published within thirty days with the voting record attached, lodged with IASRM, and offered to member societies for endorsement. Signatories are listed on this site.

It is a document with no force of law. Its only weight is that a named professional put their name to it in public, in a room where the arguments against it had been heard.
